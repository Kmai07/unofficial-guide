THE REAL GUIDE TO LIVING OFF CAMPUS AT UST
By a UST senior, published spring 2024

So you've decided to move off campus. Congratulations — you're about to learn more about adult life than any class will teach you. Here's what nobody officially tells you.

WHEN TO START LOOKING

Start looking in January for the following fall. Seriously. The best apartments near campus — particularly the ones on Fairmount, Goodrich, and the quieter stretches of Grand Ave — get snapped up by February. If you wait until March, your options are what's left over, which usually means either overpaying or accepting something with known problems.

HOW FAR IS TOO FAR

"Walking distance" to UST is generally considered anything under 15 minutes on foot. Here's my rough breakdown by neighborhood:

Summit Ave (north of Grand): 8–12 min walk. Premium pricing, beautiful street, busy on weekends.
Grand Ave (between Fairview and Snelling): 10–15 min walk. Best combination of location and amenities.
Fairmount Ave / Goodrich Ave: 12–18 min walk. Quiet, residential, genuinely underrated.
Cleveland Ave (south of Summit): 15–22 min walk. Cheaper, older buildings, more trade-offs.
Highland Park: 20–35 min walk (or 20 min bus). Good value, peaceful, less walkable to campus.

WHAT UTILITIES ARE ACTUALLY INCLUDED

This is the trap that catches first-time renters. Always, always ask what is and isn't included:

Heat included: This is massive in Minnesota. A separately-metered apartment in an old building can run $120–200/month extra in January–March. If heat is included, add that amount to your mental price comparison.
Water and sewer: Usually included in St. Paul, but confirm.
Electricity: Almost never included. Budget $40–80/month depending on unit size.
Internet: Not included, obviously. Budget $50–70/month. Ask if the building has any bulk deal.
Parking: Often not included even if the building has a lot. See the parking section above.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR ON TOURS

Here's my actual checklist for apartment tours:

1. Pull open every cabinet under the sinks (bathroom and kitchen). Look for water staining, mold, or any soft spots in the wood.
2. Run all faucets for 30 seconds. Check water pressure. Check water color.
3. Ask how many units share a boiler, and when the boiler was last serviced.
4. Test the exhaust fan in the bathroom. Weak fans = moisture buildup = mold risk.
5. Flush the toilet. Listen for running water — a constantly running toilet runs up a water bill you might be responsible for.
6. Check all windows for drafts. Hold your hand near the edges. Old windows in Minnesota can be brutal.
7. Ask who handles maintenance and how requests are submitted. "Text the landlord" is a red flag. A ticketing system or dedicated maintenance line is better.
8. Walk outside and check the parking situation yourself — don't just take their word for it.

SIGNING THE LEASE

Never sign without reading the entire lease. Key things to note in Minnesota:

Security deposit: Cannot exceed one month's rent in Minnesota.
Lease break: Does the landlord allow subletting? If you need to leave early, what are the terms?
Pets: If you have or plan to get a pet, get this in writing before signing.
Rent increases: Minnesota has no rent control. Ask about historical increases. A building with 3–5% annual increases is normal; 10–15% is a red flag.
Move-in checklist: Always document the condition of your unit with photos and video on move-in day, even if the landlord doesn't ask you to. Email yourself a copy to create a timestamp.

PROPERTY MANAGEMENT COMPANIES TO KNOW NEAR UST

Sievert Properties: Manages several buildings in Mac-Groveland. Reputation is solid — maintenance response is 3–7 days for non-urgent, faster for urgent. Reasonably responsive to email.
Harbor Bay Management: Mixed-to-negative reputation from students I've talked to. Slow maintenance, hard to reach by phone. One friend waited 6 weeks for a broken heater repair in November.
Individual landlords: Outcomes vary wildly. Some are excellent — an on-site landlord on Cleveland Ave has gotten great reviews from friends. Others are essentially unreachable. Research specific addresses on ApartmentRatings.com before signing.

MOVE-OUT AND GETTING YOUR DEPOSIT BACK

Start documenting early. In your last month:
Take video walkthrough of the entire unit.
Clean thoroughly — not just surface clean, but baseboards, inside appliances, window tracks.
Request a move-out inspection with the landlord present.
Follow up in writing if your deposit isn't returned within 21 days (Minnesota law requires return within 21 days of lease end plus a written itemization of any deductions).

If you have a dispute, Minnesota's tenant rights resources through Tenants Together or Legal Aid are genuinely helpful.
